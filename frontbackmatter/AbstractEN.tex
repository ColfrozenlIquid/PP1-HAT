	\chapter*{Abstract}
		In systems programming, balancing performance with safety and developer productivity is increasingly critical. This paper compares Rust and C++ by examining their memory management paradigms—Rust’s compile‐time ownership model versus C++’s manual memory handling—and evaluating their impact on software safety, execution speed, and developer experience. Through theoretical analysis, benchmarking tests, and real-world case studies, our investigation reveals that Rust provides comparable performance and enhanced memory safety while offering an engaging developer experience. Despite its steeper learning curve and challenges in legacy integration, Rust emerges as a promising alternative to C++, suggesting that its broader adoption could lead to safer and more maintainable systems software.
